The warped city

Redraw /*__CITY_NAME__*/ so that distance on the page is climbing cost instead of geography. Places separated by a ridge move apart; places joined by a flat corridor pull together.

One landmass is redrawn at a time: the one your origin is on or, with no origin set, the one in the middle of the map. There is no walking or cycling cost from one landmass to another to draw, so the others stay where geography puts them, greyed out. Choose an origin elsewhere to warp that landmass instead.

Flatness weight λ
0 is plain walking distance; 1 is the balanced objective; higher values make hills loom larger. Keep it modest to avoid absurd detours.
Morph
Street vertices, neighbourhood outlines, corridors and your route on that landmass are all carried by the same deformation, so the route you see is the route you would walk. The deformation is fitted to some 200 anchor junctions, street corners on a lattice sized to the landmass, so it is finer on Hong Kong Island than across Kowloon and the New Territories. Do not expect it to look like a map: on Hong Kong Island the flat north shore, cheap to walk along, gathers in the middle, and the hills and the south side fan out around it.

Layers

Street gradient key

Climb figures are cumulative elevation gain, not the net difference between the endpoints. Footbridges and flyovers are taken as level between their ends: the terrain model is the ground beneath them. Zoomed out, only the longer streets and paths are drawn; the short ones come in as you zoom. Clicking away from your route inspects the nearest street. Leaflet and the whole street graph are embedded in this file and there is no online base map, so everything works offline.
