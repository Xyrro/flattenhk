      How this works

      Routes are computed in your browser over /*__N_EDGES__*/ street and path segments covering the whole of Hong Kong, with the government's 0.5 m LiDAR terrain model (CEDD, 2020) on the Overture / OpenStreetMap street graph. The slider runs the whole trade-off between distance and climbing: every route nothing else beats on both, from the shortest to the flattest worth walking. Sliding right never shortens the route and never adds climbing. Climbing is total gain, not the difference between the endpoints. Stairs, footbridges, subways and the hillside escalators count on foot, not on a bike.

      Hong Kong Island, Kowloon with the New Territories, Lantau and the outlying islands are separate networks here: nothing crosses the water on foot or by bike but road tunnels, railways and ferries, so a route is found only within one of them. Each end of a trip is put on the nearest street corner you can use, and a place with none within 400 m is not routed to. Footbridges and flyovers are taken as level between their ends (the terrain model is the ground beneath them), and travel follows street centrelines, so which side of the road has the pavement is not modelled.

      The loop button turns the trip into a loop from one start: the slider sets the distance, dozens of loops of about that length are tried in every direction and the flattest is shown; tick allow out and back to include the same streets there and back, which is often flatter still.

      On a bike, prefer calm streets counts cycle tracks and quiet streets as shorter than they are and busy roads as longer. The slider then runs from the shortest route by that count, so the kilometres shown, which are the real ones, can fall as you slide right. Footpaths, country-park trails, road tunnels and expressways are closed to bikes, as they are by law, and the streets that are open do not all join up, so some places on one island cannot be cycled between. Where a short footpath links a cycle track and the road, a route may take it as a stretch to get off and push: it is marked (push) in the list of streets, and with calm streets preferred it counts for well over its length.

      km / mi at the top switches between kilometres and miles. GPX downloads the route on show with its elevation, for Strava, Garmin Connect, Komoot and the like. Place search is offline, in English or Chinese: the territory's street junctions, stations, estates, buildings and other places are built into this page.
